From orbit to geography

Scroll up to approach the planet, pass through its clouds and arrive at the full map at 100%. After the descent gesture ends, scroll up again to zoom into the map. Scroll down to zoom back out; at the full-map extent, keep scrolling down to return to orbit. You can also choose “Descend through the clouds.” Drag the globe to rotate it. On the surface, drag to pan and scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the location index to focus a place. Highlight a map label to inspect it; its Codex link opens the matching article. Press Escape to return to orbit, or 0 to fit the map.

The original orbital appearance comes from the Codex’s {{brand}} image saved within Kadaym Stellar Atlas v29, retrieved there on 31 October 2026. The rotating globe uses newly generated full-world artwork, with the Makal confined to one hemisphere. The clearer hemisphere is an artistic interpretation. The geographic chart is rebuilt as SVG paths traced from your supplied map, with native region polygons, labels, city markers, and shaded terrain for the Ozurzk Mountains, Mount Kadaym and the surrounding uplands. The relief is an artistic tectonic interpretation: a broad Ozurzk fold belt, an eastern coastal chain, older western uplands and an interior rift basin, with branching drainage. It is not a measured elevation model or new Codex canon.

The cloud flight is an illustrative transition from the rotating globe to the vector chart, not a georeferenced landing. The globe’s rotation speed is for display only. Locations use the positions shown on the supplied map. All labels link to matching or expected Codex articles.

This is a standalone experiment for the stellar atlas. Its globe texture, relief, vector geometry, controls and animation are bundled in the HTML download.
